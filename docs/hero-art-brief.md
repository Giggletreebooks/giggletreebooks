# Art brief — the Hero master plate

One painted illustration to replace the composited hero scene.

Everything here is measured from the built page, not estimated. Percentages
are of the hero section box, which is **1440 × 724 (1.99:1)** at a 1440px
window and **1920 × 760 (2.53:1)** on a large desktop.

---

## 1. What this artwork is for

The hero currently assembles thirteen separately-painted cut-outs — one oak,
one distant tree, one cloud, one bush, one barn — scaled, mirrored, tinted and
lit by CSS. It has been pushed as far as compositing goes. It cannot reach
"one continuous painted illustration" because the trees only have one
silhouette, the sky only has one cloud, and the barn was painted on white with
nothing around it.

This plate replaces all of that with a single scene painted in one pass, in
one light, by one hand.

**It is a background plate, not a picture.** Text, the animated logo, the
buttons and a few near-field pieces sit on top of it. Design for that.

---

## 2. The single most important constraint

The UI covers most of the canvas. Measured:

| Element | Horizontal | Vertical |
|---|---|---|
| Headline, body copy, buttons | **11.7% → 42.1%** | **23.2% → 79.6%** |
| Animated logo plate | **47.7% → 88.3%** | **18.8% → 83.4%** |

Together they occupy 8–90% of the width and 15–86% of the height.

**So the painting is read as a ring**: the sky band across the top, the ground
band across the bottom, the outer margins left and right, and the vertical
gap between the copy and the logo around 42–48%. The middle is largely hidden.

This is not a problem, it is the composition. Put the craft in the ring. Keep
the centre calm, low-contrast and uncluttered — anything detailed there is
work nobody sees, and anything high-contrast there fights the type.

---

## 3. Format and crops

**Master: 21:9, 4K.** Wider than any breakpoint needs, so every screen crops
in rather than stretching.

The plate is applied bottom-anchored and cover-fitted. Three crops must all
hold:

| Breakpoint | Visible slice of the master |
|---|---|
| Large desktop (1920) | ~full width, 2.53:1 |
| Laptop (1440) | centre ~85%, 1.99:1 |
| Tablet (834, stacked layout) | centre ~45% |
| Phone (414, stacked layout) | centre ~22%, and mostly the lower half |

**Consequence:** the framing trees at the extreme left and right edges will be
cropped away on phones — that is correct and intended. But the **horizon, the
path, and the barn must all survive the centre-22% crop.** Nothing essential
may live only at the edges.

---

## 4. Camera and horizon

- **Eye level, standing in the meadow.** Not a bird's eye, not a worm's eye.
  The visitor is standing in the world, not looking down at a map of it.
- Roughly a 35–40mm equivalent. Gentle, natural perspective. No wide-angle
  bowing, no fisheye.
- **Horizon at 62% of the plate height.** Low enough to leave a generous sky
  for the headline and the logo to sit against; high enough that the ground
  has room for a foreground.
- The ground plane recedes toward the horizon with real perspective — the
  path narrows, the grass texture tightens, the trees shrink with distance.

---

## 5. Composition and the eye path

The intended read, in order:

**Warm sky → headline (left) → down the copy → CTA → across to the logo →
down to the path → path recedes into the world.**

The path is the device that makes the last step happen. It should feel like an
invitation to walk in.

**Layout, left to right:**

- **0–10%** — a large foreground tree, cropped by the frame edge. Trunk and
  lower canopy only; the top of the tree is out of frame. This is the left
  wall of the composition.
- **10–42%** — the quiet zone. Open meadow and soft distance behind where the
  copy sits. Gentle, unfussy. Low contrast. This must not compete with type.
- **35–50%** — **the barn**, in the midground, sitting just below the horizon.
  Small — reads as a few fields away. This is the one storytelling beat that
  lands in the gap between the copy and the logo, so it must be legible in
  that narrow strip.
- **48–88%** — behind the logo plate. Sky above the horizon, meadow below.
  Deliberately simple: this is almost entirely covered.
- **88–100%** — a second foreground tree, cropped by the right edge, smaller
  and differently shaped than the left one. The right wall.
- **Bottom 16%** — the foreground band: near grass, a scatter of wildflowers,
  the widest part of the path. This is the most detailed, most saturated,
  most contrasty part of the whole plate.

---

## 6. The path

The single most important element after the light.

- Enters the frame at the **bottom edge, around 30–40% width**, at its widest
  — wide enough to read as a path you could step onto.
- Curves **rightward and back**, an S-bend, not a straight line.
- Narrows as it recedes and disappears **at the horizon around 46–52% width**,
  near the barn.
- Warm sandy earth, soft-edged, with grass encroaching at its margins. Not a
  hard-edged road.
- It must survive the phone crop, which is why it resolves near centre.

---

## 7. The barn

- Midground, **35–50% width**, base sitting on the ground plane just below the
  horizon at roughly **58–64% height**.
- Small. Distance is the point — this is the farm the stories happen on, seen
  from across the fields, not a building we are standing next to.
- **Integrated, not placed.** A hedge, long grass or a low shrub must overlap
  its base. A tree or two behind and beside it, breaking its silhouette. A
  fence line running away from it into the field.
- Warm weathered red-brown, greyed by distance — closer to the hills in value
  than to the foreground.

---

## 8. Trees

Every tree rooted. No trunk ends in mid-air; grass, undergrowth or terrain
takes the base of each one.

- **Two foreground trees**, cropped by the left and right frame edges,
  **different species, different silhouettes, different heights.** These are
  the only high-contrast, high-detail trees in the plate.
- **A midground cluster** behind and around the barn — 4–6 trees, varied,
  overlapping into a mass rather than standing as separate objects.
- **A distant treeline** along the horizon, reduced almost to a soft silhouette
  band, hue shifted toward the sky.
- **No repetition.** No two trees the same shape, spacing or size. Irregular
  gaps. This is the specific failure of the current composite and the main
  reason we are commissioning the plate.

---

## 9. Sky

- **Painted atmosphere, not placed clouds.** A soft graded wash: warm cream
  and pale gold near the horizon, lifting to a gentle, slightly cooler
  green-blue at the top.
- **Two or three cloud forms at most**, large, soft-edged, unequal, sitting in
  the air rather than on top of it. Wispier and smaller toward the horizon.
- Generous empty sky. Breathing room is the point — the headline and the logo
  both sit against it.
- Faint warm light bloom around the sun position (see below), no visible sun
  disc, no hard-edged rays.

---

## 10. Lighting

**Warm spring morning. Low sun, upper left, out of frame.**

This is non-negotiable and it must be consistent across every element,
because a single shared light source is the whole reason for commissioning
this plate.

- Warm golden light rakes in from the **upper left**.
- **Lit faces** of trunks, the barn and the foreground grass are on their
  left/upper-left sides, warm and slightly desaturated toward gold.
- **Shadows fall down and to the right**, long and soft, with cool
  green-blue bounce in them. No black. No hard edges.
- Overall contrast is gentle. This is diffuse morning light, not midday.
- A soft warm bloom in the upper-left quadrant where the light enters.

The CSS already applies a matching light pass over the whole scene. When the
plate lands with its light baked in, that pass gets reduced to a whisper —
they must agree in direction, or the scene will read as lit twice.

---

## 11. Atmosphere and depth

Depth comes from **aerial perspective**, not from blur.

- Foreground: full saturation, warm, crisp, most detail.
- Midground: slightly lifted in value, softer, less contrast.
- Distance: strongly lifted toward the sky colour, low saturation, minimal
  detail, edges dissolving.
- A soft band of morning haze pooling along the base of the distant hills.

Nothing in the plate should be uniformly sharp from front to back.

---

## 12. Palette

Warm, natural, premium. Nothing saturated or primary.

| Role | Direction |
|---|---|
| Sky | warm cream at horizon → soft cool green-blue above |
| Sunlight | pale gold |
| Foliage | soft sage, olive, muted forest green |
| Ground | warm sand, dry ochre, soft moss |
| Barn | weathered warm red-brown, greyed by distance |
| Shadow | cool green-grey, never black |

It must sit against the page's warm paper `#fffcf6`, and alongside the brand
green `#19680F` and brown `#3B1C00` used in type and buttons.

---

## 13. What must NOT be painted into the plate

These stay separate so they can parallax and animate independently. If they
are baked in, they freeze.

- Falling leaves
- Butterflies
- Pollen and light motes
- Birds
- Any near-field grass or foliage intended to cross in front of the logo

---

## 14. Separate foreground assets (delivered alongside)

Each a **transparent PNG, painted in the same light as the plate** — warm
upper-left, cool shadow lower-right. This is the detail that usually gets
missed and it is what makes them belong.

| Asset | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `fg-foliage-left` | ~1200×700 | A near-field frond/grass cluster for the bottom-left corner, cropped by the frame. Sits in front of everything, parallaxes most. |
| `fg-foliage-right` | ~1000×600 | Same for the bottom-right. Different plant, different shape. |
| `fg-branch` | ~1400×600 | A leafy branch entering from the top-left corner, to break the top edge and cross in front of the sky. |
| `leaf` ×3 | ~300 each | Three distinct leaves for the falling animation. We currently have one, which is why they repeat. |
| `butterfly` ×2 | ~300 each | Two, wings open, different colouring. |
| `bird` ×2 | ~400 each | Simple side-on gliding silhouettes, soft-edged. Currently vector and the last unfinished thing in the sky. |

---

## 15. Delivery

- Master plate: **WebP, 21:9, ~3000px wide**, no alpha, quality ~85.
- Foreground pieces: **transparent WebP**, trimmed to their own edges — no
  empty margin, or they render half-size in their boxes.
- The plate's **top edge will be faded to nothing in CSS** so it meets the
  masthead paper without a line. Paint the top band accordingly: sky only, no
  detail in the top 6%.
- The plate's **bottom edge meets the next chapter**. Keep the bottom 4% free
  of anything that would look cut.

---

## 16. Generation prompt

Ready to run against `nano-banana-2` at 21:9, 4K. Expect several attempts —
the reserved zones are the part a generator honours least, and we may need to
extend the sky or nudge the barn afterwards.

> A single continuous children's storybook illustration of a peaceful spring
> countryside at morning, hand-painted gouache and soft watercolour with
> visible brush texture, warm and gentle, premium picture-book quality, no
> text, no lettering, no characters, no people, no animals.
>
> Eye-level view standing in a meadow, natural 35mm perspective, horizon low
> at about two thirds down the frame. Low warm morning sun off the upper left,
> outside the frame: golden light rakes across from the left, soft shadows
> fall to the right, cool green-grey bounce in the shadows, gentle contrast,
> no harsh light, a soft warm bloom in the upper-left sky.
>
> A large leafy tree at the far left edge, cropped by the frame, and a second
> different smaller tree at the far right edge, also cropped — they frame the
> scene like curtains. Between them the meadow is open, calm and uncluttered.
> A soft sandy footpath enters wide at the bottom of the frame near the
> centre-left, curves in a gentle S to the right, narrows with distance and
> disappears at the horizon near the middle of the frame. A small weathered
> red-brown barn sits in the midground just left of centre, small with
> distance, its base hidden behind a hedge and long grass, with a few varied
> trees clustered behind it and a low fence running away into the field.
>
> Behind that, rolling green hills fading into soft morning haze, and a
> distant treeline reduced to a pale silhouette. Above, a wide painted sky:
> warm cream and pale gold near the horizon lifting to soft cool green-blue,
> with two or three large soft unequal clouds and plenty of empty sky.
>
> Rich detailed foreground along the bottom edge: near grass, scattered
> wildflowers, warm and saturated, dissolving into softness with distance.
> Aerial perspective throughout — foreground crisp and warm, middle distance
> softer, far distance pale and low in contrast.
>
> Muted natural palette: soft sage and olive greens, warm cream, pale gold,
> warm sand, weathered red-brown. Nothing saturated, nothing primary.

## 17. What changes in the code when it lands

Recorded so the rebuild is a known quantity rather than a rediscovery.

1. The `forest` environment drops to almost nothing: the plate replaces
   `softHills`, `treeline`, `storybookTrees`, `bushes`, `rocks`, `path`,
   `grass`, `flowers`, `barn`, `fence`, `clouds`, `sun` and `haze`.
2. `[data-chapter-light]` reduces to a whisper — the plate carries its own
   light and two light passes will fight.
3. Parallax moves from thirteen layers to three: plate (slow), mid foliage,
   near foliage (fastest).
4. The `rooted` masking, `varied()` and the scenery-resolution convention stay
   — they still serve the other chapters, which keep the composite approach
   until they get plates of their own.
5. The hero's `--clearing-x` washing and the card's inner feather stay: the
   logo still has to sit inside the painting.

The other five chapters are unaffected. This plate is the hero only.
